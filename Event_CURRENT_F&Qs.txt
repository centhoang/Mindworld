📌 FAQ — Getting started

What are we building?
Your project must use Meta models and address one of these challenges:
• Build for Your World: Solve a problem you know firsthand.
• Push the Frontier: Bring new capabilities to software, robotics, science, manufacturing, energy or other real-world systems.

Which models can I use?
The guide lists Muse Spark, Muse Wonder, Muse Image, Muse Voice Transcribe and SAM 3.1 through Meta Model API, plus open-weight Muse Glimmer and Muse Spark Open. Muse Code is also available to help you code.

Can I enter solo?
Yes! Teams can have up to 4 people. Introduce yourself in ⁠intros and find teammates in ⁠team-search.

What are the key dates?
All times are Eastern Time (ET).
• Oct 26, noon: Kickoff.
• Oct 27–Nov 4: Building period.
• Nov 4, 9 PM: Submission deadline.
• Nov 5–8: Judging.
• Nov 9, noon: Closing ceremony, live finals and main winners.
• Nov 16: Community vote winner announced.
🛠️ FAQ — Project rules

Can I build on an existing project or startup?
Yes, but your submission and demo must clearly identify—and only showcase—the features, code and functionality built during the hackathon. Unclear original contributions can lead to disqualification.

Does my code need to be public?
Yes. Your repository must be publicly accessible.

What projects should I avoid?
The guide specifically lists:
• AI medical advice, mental-health advisors and skin-condition detectors.
• Basic RAG apps, simple image analyzers and generic education chatbots.
• Job-application screeners, nutrition coaches and personality analyzers.
• Sports analyzers or coaches.
• Anything political or involving war.

Projects that violate legal, ethical or platform policies, or use code, data or assets without permission, will be disqualified. Check with @CV if you’re unsure about your idea.

How will projects be judged?
First, projects must fit the theme and meaningfully use Meta Model API and/or Meta open-weight models. Eligible entries are then scored equally on:
• Impact
• Use of Meta AI tooling
• Technical depth and execution
• Demo quality

The top 5 projects per competition track advance to the finals.
💳 FAQ — Credits and support

Do participants get API credits?
Accepted participants receive $150 in Meta Model API credits, claimed individually—not on behalf of teammates. Claim by Nov 4, 9 PM ET. Ask @CV in ⁠unknown for the claim form.

When will credits arrive?
Credits are distributed in batches. Check your account’s Billing page before using them.

Do I need a credit card?
The guide says a card is required to use Meta API credits and make API calls. Monitor your usage: spending beyond the credits may incur charges. Don’t assume a “Spend Limit” notification automatically stops billing.

How do I claim my $150 credits, or check a claim that hasn’t arrived?
Complete the Meta Model API credit claim form. If you submitted it but haven’t received credits, please double-check:
Email: Use the same email you entered when applying to the hackathon. We match claims against the approved-participant list. Only approved participants are eligible.
Team ID: Open your account at dev.meta.ai and find team_id= in the browser URL. Submit only the digits immediately after team_id=, stopping before any & or other URL text. Do not submit the full URL, team_id=, a Project ID, an API key or your hackathon team name. Solo participants have a Team ID too.
Confirmations: Complete the required terms agreement in the form.

If you entered incorrect information, resubmit with the correct details. For processing time and eligibility, see the Hackathon Credit Terms, particularly Sections 1–3.

Start with billing setup instructions and the payment options displayed in your account. For rejected cards, check the payment-failure guidance.
